\begin{abstract}
Camera-derived respiratory motion should not be forced into a single sinusoid.
We study the current reproducible state of PARH-OSSM, a physiology-aligned
harmonic state-space decomposition that separates respiratory motion into a
second-order oscillatory component, a baseline trend, and an aperiodic
residual. The audited implementation is an eight-dimensional exactly linear
state-space model conditioned on an external frequency estimate, and it stores
dual outputs for oscillatory analysis ($z_{\mathrm{osc}}$) and full waveform
analysis ($z_{\mathrm{full}}$), together with causal and Rauch--Tung--Striebel
smoothed trajectories and per-sample diagnostics.

This manuscript reports only claims supported by persistent artifacts in the
current repository. A complete six-family COHFACE rerun is now available and
is used for all quantitative tables. The MAHNOB-HCI production run is still
incomplete, so no MAHNOB quantitative claims are made here. The live COHFACE
ladder now includes raw OF, an additional OF-derived displacement bridge
(\texttt{OF\_bridge}), DoF, and three Profile1D families. On COHFACE, PARH-OSSM now
exceeds KFstd in waveform fidelity on raw OF, is effectively tied on
P1D-linear, slightly exceeds KFstd on P1D-quadratic in waveform CCC while
remaining worse in waveform MAE/DTW, and still trails on \texttt{OF\_bridge},
P1D-cubic, and DoF. In frequency tracking, PARH-OSSM improves over KFstd in
five of the six families after routing rate metrics through the stored
\texttt{track\_hz}, but the raw base observation remains the strongest absolute
rate route on raw OF, \texttt{OF\_bridge}, and all three Profile1D families.
Regenerated calibration diagnostics from the saved PARH-OSSM arrays yield
median NIS means of roughly 0.83--0.94, median NIS in-band fractions of
roughly 0.93--0.96, and Student-$t$ weights that remain close to one,
indicating conservative but internally consistent scaling rather than strong
posterior downweighting. Mechanism audits from the saved per-frame diagnostics
now show moderate dynamical-novelty scores, non-saturated oscillatory-support
scores, and a family-dependent residual-energy share, indicating that the
current implementation is no longer a nearly dormant decomposition scaffold.

We therefore position the current system as a physiology-aligned,
self-auditing scaffold whose decomposition and traceability are already
useful, but whose main bottleneck is still the observation model. The
repository now contains multiple gated observation-calibration prototypes, and
the current evidence supports two promotable observation-construction steps:
a family-selective harmonic-only observation row for P1D-quadratic and
P1D-cubic, and an additional OF-derived displacement-compatible family
(\texttt{OF\_bridge}) that improves OF-family rate tracking. Additional broad
calibration variants, helper-only, helper-blend, and aggressive OF
output-side policies all failed promotion.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Introduction}
Video-based respiratory monitoring offers a contactless alternative to belts,
thermistors, and capnography in settings where unobtrusive sensing is valuable,
including sleep analysis, neonatal monitoring, and general bedside
observation~\cite{Bartula2013,Tarassenko2014,Janssen2016}. Most video pipelines,
however, still collapse the task to scalar rate estimation: a motion proxy is
bandpass filtered and a dominant frequency is extracted by spectral or
autocorrelation analysis~\cite{Li2014optical,Bai2021,Welch1967}. That framing
is often adequate for stable recordings, but it is too restrictive for
waveform morphology, baseline drift, and irregular events.

Two adjacent literature blocks motivate the present reset. First, state-space
methods are only as meaningful as the correspondence between the chosen latent
state, the observation equation, and the structure of the system that is
believed to generate the data~\cite{Harvey1989,DurbinKoopman2012,Sarkka2013}.
That principle is especially important here because camera-derived respiration
is not measured directly; it is observed through projection, extractor-family
choice, and preprocessing. Recent benchmark work on RGB-camera respiration also
shows that motion-based families, rPPG-based families, and learned pipelines
behave very differently across datasets and evaluation targets~\cite{Boccignone2025Review}.
Second, robust and adaptive filtering methods improve state estimation under
outliers or changing noise statistics, for example through covariance
adaptation, heavy-tailed likelihoods, or both~\cite{Mehra1970,MohamedSchwarz1999,Piche2012,Roth2017,Huang2017}.
Third, oscillator state-space models provide a principled way to represent
quasi-periodic latent structure while preserving tractable inference under
conditional linearity~\cite{Sarkka2012DRIFTER,Somata2018,Sarkka2013,SolinSarkka2014}.
Those lines of work are relevant but not sufficient on their own for
camera-derived respiration, because they do not by themselves specify how
respiratory morphology, baseline drift, and non-oscillatory event structure
should be separated in the latent representation. Learned monitoring
approaches can also be effective~\cite{Chaichulee2017,Perepelkina2020}, but
they do not remove the need for an interpretable model of what portion of the
motion trace should be treated as oscillatory versus aperiodic.

The central premise of this work is that camera-derived respiratory motion is
not a single clean sinusoid. It contains at least four interpretable parts:
an oscillatory respiratory drive, harmonic asymmetry within each breath,
baseline or projection drift, and residual structure that is respiratory but
not well described by a narrow-band oscillator. A physiology-aligned model
should separate those roles explicitly instead of forcing all mismatch into one
innovation-magnitude logic. This premise is also consistent with respiratory
physiology literature, which treats breathing as a variable pattern shaped by
rhythm generation, mechanical feedback, non-respiratory motor acts, and
behavioral or emotional modulation rather than as a perfectly stationary
sinusoid~\cite{Oku2022,vanDenBosch2021}.

This manuscript intentionally reports the \emph{current verified state} of
PARH-OSSM rather than an aspirational final system. A reverse-audit of the
repository, saved results, and the previous manuscript showed that the older
NAROSSM framing overclaimed what the present code and result files support. We
therefore reset the paper around persistent evidence only.

The present manuscript makes three contributions.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item It defines the current minimal PARH-OSSM v1 as an eight-dimensional
      harmonic decomposition with dual outputs, exact linear filtering, and
      stored per-sample diagnostics.
\item It reports the current reproducible evidence boundary: a completed
      COHFACE production run, saved diagnostic PKLs, and an incomplete MAHNOB
      production run that is not yet used for benchmark claims.
\item It explains why corrected rate evaluation reveals a real PARH-OSSM rate
      gain over KFstd on COHFACE, while waveform gains remain limited despite
      the additional model structure.
\end{itemize}

The manuscript therefore proceeds in three layers. It first defines the
code-verified model structure and its intended metric routing. It then reports
only those quantitative results that are traceable to persistent artifacts.
Finally, it interprets those artifacts mechanistically, including why the
current COHFACE behavior is informative but still insufficient to stabilise the
final paper story without rerun evidence from the updated code and from
MAHNOB-HCI.

\section*{PARH-OSSM v1}
\subsection*{Latent decomposition}
The current audited model uses the latent state
\begin{equation}
\state_t =
\left[
h_{c,t}^{(1)},\,
h_{s,t}^{(1)},\,
h_{c,t}^{(2)},\,
h_{s,t}^{(2)},\,
b_t,\,
\dot{b}_t,\,
r_t,\,
\dot{r}_t
\right]^\top,
\end{equation}
where $(h^{(1)}, h^{(2)})$ are the fundamental and second-harmonic oscillatory
components, $(b_t,\dot{b}_t)$ is a slow baseline block, and
$(r_t,\dot{r}_t)$ is an aperiodic residual block. The observation equation is
\begin{equation}
\obs_t = H \state_t + v_t, \qquad
H = [1,\,0,\,1,\,0,\,1,\,0,\,1,\,0].
\end{equation}

The harmonic blocks use rotation-matrix dynamics conditioned on an external
frequency estimate, the baseline block follows a constant-velocity model, and
the residual block follows a damped random walk. Because the model remains
linear conditional on the external frequency, standard Kalman filtering and
Rauch--Tung--Striebel smoothing remain valid~\cite{Kalman1960,Sarkka2013,RauchTungStriebel1965}.

\subsection*{Dual outputs}
The current implementation stores two outputs:
\begin{equation}
z_{\mathrm{osc},t} = h_{c,t}^{(1)} + h_{c,t}^{(2)},
\qquad
z_{\mathrm{full},t} = z_{\mathrm{osc},t} + b_t + r_t.
\end{equation}
$z_{\mathrm{osc}}$ is the intended oscillatory output for rate and dominant
rhythm analysis. $z_{\mathrm{full}}$ is the intended full respiratory motion
estimate for waveform fidelity and morphology. Both causal and smoothed
versions are stored in the saved trial payloads.

\subsection*{Why this decomposition is physiology-aligned}
The model structure is not arbitrary. A second harmonic is included because
respiratory motion is usually asymmetric over each breath cycle even when the
dominant rate is stable; forcing that asymmetry into a purely sinusoidal state
invites harmonic confusion and pushes morphology into innovation residuals.
The baseline block is needed because camera-derived chest motion carries slow
projection drift and posture-related movement that are not themselves the
respiratory rhythm. The residual block exists for a different reason: it is the
state-space location where irregular respiratory content should live when the
oscillator support weakens, including amplitude transients or breath-shape
departures that are still respiratory but not well explained by the harmonic
submodel.

This decomposition also clarifies the evaluation policy. Rate metrics should be
computed from $z_{\mathrm{osc}}$, because rate is an oscillatory quantity.
Waveform-fidelity metrics should be computed from $z_{\mathrm{full}}$, because
the full motion trace is the object of comparison when baseline and aperiodic
content matter. That separation is one of the most important paper-level
distinctions from the earlier manuscript state, where rate and waveform
interpretations were more easily conflated. It also follows directly from the
state-space design principle above: once the state variables are assigned
distinct physiological roles, the observation targets and evaluation metrics
must remain aligned with those roles rather than collapsing everything back to
a single generic output.

\subsection*{Disentangled adaptation in the current code}
The current scaffold separates three internal scores:
\begin{equation}
q_{\mathrm{obs},t} \rightarrow \pi_t,\qquad
q_{\mathrm{dyn},t} \rightarrow Q_{\mathrm{osc},t},\qquad
1-q_{\mathrm{osc},t} \rightarrow Q_{\mathrm{aper},t}.
\end{equation}
Here $\pi_t$ is a prior trust term used through
\(
\Reff = R_t / \pi_t
\),
while Student-$t$ downweighting remains a posterior safeguard through
$\lambda_t$. In the current code, Mehra-style adaptive measurement noise
remains the primary scale-setting mechanism~\cite{Mehra1970}; the quality
scores modulate the prior trust and process noise around that baseline.

\subsection*{Current implementation boundary}
The present repository already contains the following verified features: the
eight-dimensional state, $K=2$ harmonic structure, baseline and residual
blocks, dual outputs, warm-up-only frequency initialisation, external helper
frequency updates, causal and smoothed outputs, and saved per-sample
diagnostics for $R_t$, $\pi_t$, $\lambda_t$, $\nu_t$,
$q_{\mathrm{obs},t}$, $q_{\mathrm{dyn},t}$, and $q_{\mathrm{osc},t}$.

Several important items are still incomplete or unstable. First, the current
live production path is still not a fully general observation-calibrated
state-space model. Instead, the code now uses a family-selective hybrid:
raw OF uses a light observation path, \texttt{OF\_bridge} is added as an
OF-derived displacement-compatible observation family, DoF remains on the
legacy stack, P1D-linear remains on the legacy stack with selective frequency
rescue, and P1D-quadratic/cubic now use a bounded warm-up observation-row
calibration.
Second, broader warm-up observation-calibration prototypes and helper-only or
helper-blend observation-routing variants failed the same 12-trial COHFACE
promotion gate. This means preprocessing-policy switching alone is no longer a
credible primary redesign axis. Third, the calibration CSV now emits NIS and
lambda aggregates for PARH-OSSM from saved diagnostics arrays, but comparable
per-frame calibration fields are still unavailable for KFstd. Fourth, the
all-family COHFACE rerun has now been completed under the current live
default including \texttt{OF\_bridge}, but the MAHNOB-HCI production run is still
incomplete, so the present manuscript does not claim cross-dataset results.

\subsection*{Observation-family semantics in the live scaffold}
The most important implementation lesson so far is that the observation family
is part of the model, not just part of preprocessing. Table~\ref{tab:obs_family_map}
therefore summarises the six active COHFACE families by their current
construction, observation domain, and present PARH role. Two contrasts matter
most. First, raw OF and \texttt{OF\_bridge} are both derived from optical flow
but they are not interchangeable: raw OF remains a helper-heavy velocity-like
family, whereas \texttt{OF\_bridge} is a displacement-compatible constructed
family whose main present benefit is rate. Second, the Profile1D quadratic and
cubic routes are not just ``better smoothed inputs''; they are currently the
waveform-primary harmonic families because their observation semantics align
best with the shared latent decomposition. This is the core reviewer-facing
point of the present redesign: the same PARH latent state is kept across
families, but what is observed and how it should be interpreted is explicitly
family-dependent.

\section*{Results}
\subsection*{Evidence boundary}
All quantitative claims in this manuscript are restricted to persistent files
generated by the completed all-family COHFACE rerun in
\texttt{results/20260408\_cohface\_prod\_ofbridge\_full}. The evaluation CSVs,
table-ready inputs, mechanism audit, and case-study manifest were all
regenerated from that run. The same repository also contains gate-tested
negative controls and pair-fusion experiments, but those remain secondary or
explicitly rejected design evidence rather than current production behavior.
The MAHNOB-HCI production directory still does not contain a completed metrics
directory and metadata file, so MAHNOB-HCI is discussed only as the intended
irregular-regime benchmark for the next stage, not as a current quantitative
result.

\subsection*{COHFACE rate accuracy}
Table~\ref{tab:cohface_t3} reports the current median per-trial COHFACE rate
metrics after correcting the rate-evaluation path for methods that save
\texttt{track\_hz}. This materially changes the rate interpretation relative to
the older legacy spectral path. PARH-OSSM now improves on KFstd in median MAE
and RMSE in five of the six families, and it is the strongest estimator on
DoF. The newly promoted \texttt{OF\_bridge} route is also now the best PARH family by
the locked T3 ladder rule, but even there the raw base and KFstd bridge routes
still achieve lower absolute median error. The remaining limitation is
different: the raw base observation still has the best median MAE on raw OF,
\texttt{OF\_bridge}, and all three Profile1D families.

This means the old ``PARH $\approx$ KFstd'' reading was partly an
evaluation-path artifact, not a faithful description of the saved PARH-OSSM
outputs. Once T3 is routed through the intended oscillatory output, PARH-OSSM
is rate-competitive. The unresolved issue is that this rate improvement still
does not translate into leading waveform fidelity. Figure~\ref{fig:cohface_t3_summary}
makes the rate-versus-family split explicit, especially the new
raw-OF-versus-\texttt{OF\_bridge} contrast.

\subsection*{COHFACE waveform fidelity}

locked policy Base(\texttt{signal\_hat}) / KFstd(\texttt{signal\_hat}) /

\subsection*{COHFACE mechanism audit}
To understand why PARH-OSSM now shows better T3 rate metrics than KFstd while
still falling short of KFstd on waveform fidelity, we aggregated the saved
per-sample diagnostics directly from the persistent trial PKLs of the completed
production run. Table~\ref{tab:cohface_mech} summarises the resulting
mechanism profile.

The pattern is now materially healthier than in the older conservative
scaffold. Observation trust remains only mildly reduced on average
($q_{\mathrm{obs}} \approx 0.91$--0.93). The dynamical-novelty score is no
longer near-saturated: $q_{\mathrm{dyn}}$ lies around 0.56--0.69 across
families, while $q_{\mathrm{osc}}$ now lies around 0.73--0.83 rather than
near one. The aperiodic pathway therefore opens more often than before, but
its activity is clearly family-dependent. Raw OF, \texttt{OF\_bridge}, P1D-linear,
and DoF retain non-zero residual energy, whereas the stronger
P1D-quadratic/cubic routes now drive almost all clean COHFACE structure
through the harmonic block. Strong Student-$t$ downweighting is still modest
overall, but raw OF remains the family with the clearest low-level tail
fraction.

In other words, the current implementation is no longer a nearly dormant
extension of KFstd. The extra decomposition states are being used. The
remaining limitation is that this healthier decomposition still does not close
the family-specific observation gap strongly enough to dominate the best raw
base proxies. Figure~\ref{fig:cohface_mechanism_activation} shows this
visually: the mechanism scores are active and calibrated, but the family-level
energy routing is still uneven.

\section*{Discussion}
\subsection*{Why current PARH-OSSM gains are limited on COHFACE}
After correcting the T3 evaluation path, COHFACE no longer supports the claim
that PARH-OSSM is merely tied with KFstd. The current saved PARH-OSSM outputs
are better than KFstd for rate tracking in five of the six observation
families. The remaining limitation is narrower but still important:
PARH-OSSM still fails to beat the raw base observation on rate MAE in every
family except DoF, and its waveform gains remain family-specific rather than
uniform.

The main bottleneck is now better described as an observation-model problem
than as a dormant-latent problem. The current scaffold no longer shows the old
near-saturated $q_{\mathrm{osc}}$ behavior. Instead, the saved diagnostics show
moderate $q_{\mathrm{dyn}}$, non-saturated $q_{\mathrm{osc}}$, and residual
energy shares of roughly 0.7--1.8\%. The decomposition is therefore active.
What remains unresolved is that the observation equation still does not encode
family-specific visibility, mild lag mismatch, or nuisance projection effects
well enough to consistently outperform the strongest raw proxies.

The negative gate results point in the same direction. Exposing helper-style
observations to quad/cub families slightly improves rate but slightly harms
waveform, helper routing for all P1D families clearly breaks P1D-linear, and
helper-blend policies are slightly worse than the current scaffold. That means
continued preprocessing-policy switching is not the answer. The next gain has
to come from stronger observation-row semantics rather than from another
preprocessing rearrangement. The current evidence now supports that claim in
two complementary ways: a selective harmonic-only observation row helps the
two higher-order profile families, and an OF-derived displacement bridge
changes the OF-family rate/waveform trade-off in a reproducible direction.
The resulting best-single ladder is therefore split by task:
\texttt{OF\_bridge} is now the strongest PARH T3 family under the locked
median-based ladder rule, whereas \texttt{P1D\_quadratic} remains the
strongest PARH T4 family.

\subsection*{What already matches the intended final model}
The current implementation is not a placeholder in the trivial sense. The core
architecture already matches the intended PARH-OSSM direction in several
important ways: the eight-dimensional latent decomposition is real, the
second-harmonic morphology block is active, the baseline and residual states
exist in the saved trajectories, warm-up-only initialisation is implemented,
the forward and RTS-smoothed paths are separated, and the saved result payloads
already contain the diagnostics needed for mechanism-level auditing.

That matters because the current issue is not ``the model was never built''.
The issue is that the presently implemented adaptation maps and evaluation path
do not yet exploit that structure in an intent-aligned way.

\subsection*{What remains approximate or wrong}
Several mismatches still separate the current code from the intended final
PARH-OSSM story.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item The current COHFACE rerun now reflects the separated helper path, softer
      $q_{\mathrm{dyn}}$/$q_{\mathrm{osc}}$ mappings, corrected Student-$t$
      algebra, and warm-up-only KFstd initialisation, but it still does not
      solve the observation bottleneck.
\item The calibration CSV is now informative for PARH-OSSM, but comparator
      symmetry is incomplete because KFstd does not save matching per-frame
      calibration arrays and posterior frequency uncertainty is still missing.
\item Three observation-calibration prototypes now exist in the repository,
      but the component-wise, global-scalar, and oscillator-vs-auxiliary
      variants have all failed to produce a promotable COHFACE gate result.
\item Additional helper-only and helper-blend observation-routing variants also
      failed the same gate, which means the next redesign step must move beyond
      preprocessing-policy switching.
\item The current bridge observation path is still an interim family-specific
      workaround rather than a final observation-calibrated generative model.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Limitations and next steps}
This manuscript intentionally omits several claims that were present in the
previous draft. There is no MAHNOB-HCI quantitative section because the current
production run was incomplete at audit time. The current T6 table is
PARH-specific because KFstd does not yet expose matching per-frame diagnostics.
There are no cross-dataset transfer or synthetic outlier-injection claims
because the current persistent production artifacts do not support them.

The repository now contains a live COHFACE scaffold reference, so the next
stage should prioritise four concrete tasks before the manuscript is
stabilised: (1) retain the six-family COHFACE ladder as the current evidence
boundary; (2) strengthen OF-family observation semantics beyond the present
raw-vs-bridge split while preserving waveform meaning; (3) make the residual
branch more identifiable than the current release heuristics allow; and
(4) complete the MAHNOB-HCI production run under the current code while
extending comparator-side diagnostics so calibration and uncertainty can be
compared more symmetrically. Only then will it be possible to assess whether
the decomposition truly pays off in irregular regimes rather than only in
stationary ones.

\section*{Methods}
\subsection*{Observation families}
We use six heterogeneous chest-motion proxy families that are already
implemented in the repository: raw OF-Farneb\"ack dense optical flow, an
OF-derived displacement-compatible bridge (\texttt{OF\_bridge}), DoF, and three
one-dimensional spatial profile families (linear, quadratic, and cubic).
These observation families are treated as separate single-family inference
paths rather than fused into one multi-family model in the current manuscript.
That restriction is intentional: the current evidence boundary supports fair
single-family comparison, not yet a joint-fusion claim.

These families should not be described as if they all observe the same
waveform. Raw OF-Farneb\"ack is the median vertical component of dense optical
flow between adjacent ROI frames, so it is closer to a signed oscillatory
motion or velocity-like proxy than to absolute displacement. \texttt{OF\_bridge} is
derived from raw OF through an OF-to-displacement bridge and therefore acts as
a displacement-compatible OF-family route rather than a new extractor. DoF is
a thresholded count of large inter-frame pixel changes and is therefore closer
to motion energy than to a clean respiratory waveform. The Profile1D families
estimate a frame-to-frame chest-shift surrogate from one-dimensional vertical
profiles and are therefore closer to displacement-like proxies, with the
quadratic and cubic variants exposing richer harmonic morphology than the
linear variant. The observation model should therefore use a shared latent
respiratory decomposition but family-specific observation semantics.

This also explains why the current redesign introduces an additional
\texttt{OF\_bridge} family but does not yet introduce an analogous
``DoF-bridge.'' Raw OF retains signed directional structure from a motion field,
which makes a displacement-compatible reconstruction at least identifiable in
principle. DoF does not: it is an aggregated motion-energy count after
thresholding, so its sign, phase, and direct displacement meaning are already
collapsed before state-space inference begins. Under the present code and data,
DoF is therefore better treated as a nuisance-limited auxiliary family than as
a near-displacement waveform proxy that merely awaits a bridge transform.

For that reason, preprocessing is part of the observation definition rather
than a generic denoising block. The raw extractor output, the current
inference-path signal, and the helper-path signal all have different meanings.
The current scaffold now reflects this more explicitly: raw OF uses a light
observation path, \texttt{OF\_bridge} provides a separate OF-derived
displacement-compatible route, DoF remains on the legacy path because nuisance
contamination is still too strong, P1D-linear remains a conservative
displacement-like family with selective frequency rescue, and
P1D-quadratic/cubic are the only families for which bounded observation-side
calibration has passed the COHFACE no-harm gate. Stronger relaxed OF helper
blending was also tested but failed the full-dataset confirmation step, which
reinforces the need for better OF observation semantics rather than a looser
output-side gate. The intended final model keeps the latent state shared
across families while replacing the current generic observation row with
bounded family-specific visibility and lag semantics.
The current COHFACE evidence also shows why constructed observation families
deserve to be treated as first-class model objects: raw OF and
\texttt{OF\_bridge} invert the usual rate-versus-waveform ranking across the same
underlying optical-flow source, which would be impossible to explain if
observation construction were merely a cosmetic preprocessing step.

Figure~\ref{fig:cohface_f6_failure_cases} makes two of these limitations
visible without post hoc cherry-picking because both panels are selected from
persistent manifests. The top row shows a residual-heavy raw-OF PARH case. The
residual branch is clearly active and the non-oscillatory-need trace rises, but
the waveform fit remains poor. This is exactly the current repository truth:
the residual path is no longer dormant, yet it is still not a fully
identifiable physiological branch that can rescue a strongly mismatched
observation. The bottom row shows the worst \texttt{OF\_bridge} case from the
bridge manifest. This keeps the story honest: \texttt{OF\_bridge} is a useful
new observation family for rate-oriented analysis, but it is not a universal
replacement for raw OF. That is why the paper currently promotes
\texttt{OF\_bridge} as an additional family rather than collapsing the two.

\subsection*{Datasets and current artifact status}
COHFACE contains 160 recordings from 40 subjects in a controlled
lab setting~\cite{COHFACE2017}. MAHNOB-HCI provides a more heterogeneous
emotion-elicitation environment with longer and higher-frame-rate
recordings~\cite{MAHNOB2012}. The intended final PARH-OSSM paper uses COHFACE
as a regular, do-no-harm regime and MAHNOB-HCI as an irregular regime where
the residual pathway should matter more.

At the time of the present manuscript state, the all-family COHFACE production
run including \texttt{OF\_bridge} had completed and its metrics and metadata were
available. The MAHNOB-HCI production directory still lacks the completed
metrics and metadata required for benchmark tables. We therefore restrict
quantitative claims to COHFACE. Figure~\ref{fig:dataset_observation_regime}
shows why the observation family should be treated as part of the modelled
measurement channel rather than as a generic interchangeable preprocessing
choice.

\subsection*{Evaluation protocol}
The current manuscript uses three quantitative sources.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item Table~\ref{tab:cohface_t3} comes from the regenerated
      \texttt{paper/tables\_ready/T3\_rate\_main.csv}, which is itself derived
      from the persistent \texttt{metrics\_freq\_domain\_raw.csv} file. Base
      rows use the signal-spectral fallback; KFstd and PARH-OSSM use the saved
      \texttt{track\_hz}.
\item Table~\ref{tab:cohface_t4} comes from the regenerated
      \texttt{paper/tables\_ready/T4\_waveform\_main.csv}, which is derived
      from \texttt{metrics\_waveform\_raw.csv}. Under the current locked policy,
      Base and KFstd use \texttt{signal\_hat} while PARH-OSSM uses
      \texttt{z\_full}, all in smoothed mode.
\item Table~\ref{tab:cohface_t6} comes from the regenerated
      \texttt{paper/tables\_ready/T6\_diagnostics\_main.csv}, which is derived
      from \texttt{metrics\_filter\_diagnostics\_raw.csv}. In the current
      repository state, meaningful NIS/lambda rows are available only for
      PARH-OSSM because those arrays are saved in the PARH trial payloads.
\end{itemize}

This differs intentionally from the evaluation protocol adopted in the
\texttt{resPyre} benchmark review~\cite{Boccignone2025Review}. In that benchmark,
each method is first treated as a respiratory-waveform estimator and
respiration rate is then derived from the estimated waveform by spectral peak
selection. That design is appropriate for comparing heterogeneous extractors in
a common benchmark pipeline, but it is not sufficient for the current PARH
question because it collapses oscillatory-rate behaviour and full-waveform
behaviour back onto a single output. We therefore keep the benchmark spirit
and datasets, but change the evaluation routing to match the latent semantics:
T3 uses the oscillatory output when the model provides it, T4 uses the full
output, and T6 reports calibration diagnostics separately.

The waveform metrics follow a common pipeline: bandpass filtering to the
respiratory band, per-signal z-scoring, cross-correlation alignment, then
whole-trial concordance correlation coefficient, waveform MAE, and normalised
DTW~\cite{LinCCC1989}. Calibration metrics are computed from saved per-frame
diagnostics arrays when present, with frame logs used only for supplementary
failure and uncertainty fields.

For the final submission, all overlay figures and case studies should be drawn
from a persistent case-selection manifest rather than manual inspection, and
all confirmatory method comparisons should use paired non-parametric tests on
the regenerated rerun artifacts. That policy is not yet exercised in the
current manuscript because the necessary MAHNOB-HCI rerun and ablation
artifacts are still pending, but it is already locked at the paper-planning
level to reduce reviewer exposure to cherry-picking or post hoc metric
selection.

\subsection*{Implementation audit procedure}
Beyond the table-ready CSVs, we audited the current implementation by reading
the model, wrapper, and evaluation code paths and by aggregating the saved
per-sample diagnostic arrays directly from the COHFACE production PKLs. This
allowed us to compare the intended design against the actual implementation and
to diagnose why the current COHFACE behaviour now shows family-specific gains:
better rate tracking than KFstd in most families, an observation-construction
gain for \texttt{OF\_bridge}, and still incomplete waveform dominance in the
stronger profile families.
