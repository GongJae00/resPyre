\title{A Physiology-Aligned State-Space Decomposition Framework for Camera-Derived Respiratory Motion}

\author[1]{Young-Jae Cho}
\author[2,*]{Dae-Yeol Kim}
\affil[1]{Master's Program in AI Computer Engineering, Department of Electrical, Electronic and Computer Engineering, University of Ulsan, Ulsan, Republic of Korea}
\affil[2]{School of ICT Convergence, University of Ulsan, Ulsan, Republic of Korea}
\affil[*]{daeyeol@ulsan.ac.kr}

\keywords{contactless respiration, state-space model, harmonic decomposition, adaptive observation law, camera-derived motion}

\begin{abstract}
Video-based respiration monitoring typically estimates breathing rate from
indirect camera-derived motion signals shaped by viewpoint, clothing, posture,
body motion, and preprocessing. We introduce PARH-OSSM, a physiology-aligned
state-space decomposition framework that separates camera observations into
oscillatory rate/timing, second-harmonic morphology, baseline projection drift,
and aperiodic residual components. The model uses a label-free adaptive
observation law and dual readouts: \(z_{\mathrm{osc}}\) for rate and
\(z_{\mathrm{full}}\) for waveform morphology. In a complete-cohort evaluation,
COHFACE represented a clean regime (160 trials), where PARH-OSSM achieved rate
MAE 0.335 bpm, Pearson \(r=0.855\), and aligned waveform CCC 0.859. MAHNOB-HCI
represented a harder regime (525 trials), where PARH-OSSM improved rate
tracking (MAE 2.410 bpm) but waveform recovery remained limited. The failure
taxonomy showed that many hard-regime cases were bounded by the current
observation bank rather than by gross state-space divergence. These results
establish PARH-OSSM as an auditable observation-to-state framework and
highlight the need for richer respiratory observation operators when waveform
morphology is the target.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Introduction}
Video-based respiratory monitoring offers a contactless alternative to belts,
thermistors, and capnography in settings where unobtrusive sensing is valuable,
including sleep analysis, neonatal monitoring, and general bedside
observation~\cite{Bartula2013,Tarassenko2014,Janssen2016}. Most video pipelines,
however, still collapse the task to scalar rate estimation: a motion proxy is
bandpass filtered and a dominant frequency is extracted by spectral or
autocorrelation analysis~\cite{Li2014optical,Bai2021,Welch1967}. That framing
is often adequate for stable recordings, but it is too restrictive for
waveform morphology, baseline drift, and irregular events.

This limitation is not only a filtering problem. Camera-derived respiration is
not measured directly; it is obtained through body projection, region
selection, a motion or colour operator, and subsequent preprocessing. Recent
benchmark work on RGB-camera respiration shows that motion-based methods,
rPPG-based methods, and learned pipelines can behave differently across
datasets and evaluation targets~\cite{Boccignone2025Review}. Physiologically,
visible upper-body or thoraco-abdominal displacement is often the most direct
camera cue for breathing. In practice, viewpoint, clothing, posture, body
habitus, illumination, ROI placement, and non-respiratory motion can attenuate,
invert, or remove that cue~\cite{Wang2021AlgorithmicRespiration}. A respiratory
state estimator therefore needs to account not only for temporal noise, but
also for the fact that each camera-derived motion cue is a different
observation of the underlying respiratory process.

State-space modelling provides a natural language for this problem because the
latent state, the observation equation, and the assumed generative structure can
be written explicitly~\cite{Harvey1989,DurbinKoopman2012,Sarkka2013}. Robust
and adaptive filters can further reduce the effect of outliers or changing
noise statistics through covariance adaptation and heavy-tailed
updates~\cite{Mehra1970,MohamedSchwarz1999,Piche2012,Roth2017,Huang2017}.
Oscillator state-space models are particularly suitable for quasi-periodic
physiological rhythms because they preserve tractable inference under
conditional linearity~\cite{Sarkka2012DRIFTER,Somata2018,Sarkka2013,SolinSarkka2014}.
However, these methods do not by themselves decide which part of a
camera-derived trace should represent the respiratory oscillator, which part
should represent breath-shape asymmetry, and which part should be treated as
slow projection drift or aperiodic respiratory motion. Learned monitoring
approaches can also be effective~\cite{Chaichulee2017,Perepelkina2020}, but an
interpretable decomposition remains useful when the goal is to explain why a
video contains enough respiratory information in one regime and not in another.

The central premise of this work is that camera-derived respiratory motion is
not a single clean sinusoid. It contains at least four interpretable parts:
an oscillatory respiratory drive, harmonic asymmetry within each breath,
baseline or projection drift, and residual structure that is respiratory but
not well described by a narrow-band oscillator. A physiology-aligned model
should separate those roles explicitly instead of forcing all mismatch into one
innovation-magnitude logic. This premise is also consistent with respiratory
physiology literature, which treats breathing as a variable pattern shaped by
rhythm generation, mechanical feedback, non-respiratory motor acts, and
behavioral or emotional modulation rather than as a perfectly stationary
sinusoid~\cite{Oku2022,vanDenBosch2021}.

In this study, we develop a physiology-aligned respiratory decomposition
framework, PARH-OSSM, that keeps these roles separate. We use a set of fixed
camera-motion observation classes not as a leaderboard ensemble, but as a
controlled way to ask which physical or algorithmic view of the video still
carries respiratory timing, morphology, or nuisance-dominated content. We then
connect those observations to a harmonic state-space model with separate
oscillatory, harmonic, baseline, and residual pathways, and we route rate and
waveform analyses through different readouts. The evaluation is deliberately
conservative: we report only results that are reproduced by the same
predefined protocol spanning direct observations, an OSSM-KF comparator, and
the integrated PARH-OSSM readout. PARH-OSSM itself uses Kalman filtering and
Rauch--Tung--Striebel smoothing as the inference machinery for its decomposed
state-space model. We reserve the name OSSM-KF for the stripped-down,
non-adaptive oscillator state-space comparator with a standard Kalman filter.
The distinction is therefore
not ``Kalman versus non-Kalman'', but a comparison between a fixed resonator
baseline and the full physiology-aligned observation law, state decomposition,
dual readouts, and diagnostics. The goal is not to claim universal superiority,
but to show what can be inferred when the respiratory observation is strong and
how the failure boundary appears when it is weak.

Accordingly, we make three scientific steps. We formulate camera-derived
respiratory motion as an eight-dimensional harmonic state-space decomposition
with separate oscillatory, morphology-complement, and full-waveform roles. We
evaluate complete COHFACE and MAHNOB-HCI cohorts using three predefined
routes: direct observations, the non-adaptive OSSM-KF comparator, and the full
PARH-OSSM framework. We then analyse the clean-regime and hard-regime outcomes with label-free
observability diagnostics, so that MAHNOB-HCI failures are treated as evidence
about the current camera-observation set rather than hidden as generic model
failure.

We organize the analysis around this evidence chain. Methods first define the
observation-to-state topology, latent respiratory roles, label-free reliability
law, camera-derived observation classes, and evaluation protocol. Results then
report rate, waveform, calibration, ablation, and failure-taxonomy evidence
across the clean COHFACE regime and the harder MAHNOB-HCI regime before the
Discussion interprets the remaining observation bottleneck.

\section*{Methods}
\subsection*{Framework overview and observation-to-state topology}
We define the method in the same left-to-right order as the topology in
Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}: camera-derived respiratory evidence,
candidate views and label-free summaries, adaptive observation law and
role-specific weights, the PARH-OSSM latent respiratory state, decoupled
readouts, diagnostics, and finally the evaluation protocol used to generate
the reported tables and figures.

Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} summarizes the observation-to-state topology.

Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} should be read as a role-allocation diagram rather
than as a linear preprocessing recipe. The left side shows that several fixed
motion operators can expose different fragments of the same underlying
respiratory process. The middle blocks show where the label-free evidence is
converted into trust, effective noise, process scales, and role-specific

diagnostic outputs are therefore used to audit observability and consistency,
not to create an unreported feedback path.

The following subsections use the same hierarchy. They first define how video
is converted into a shared pool of candidate respiratory evidence, then how
label-free reliability and role weights route that evidence into the latent
state, and finally how the two readouts and diagnostics are evaluated.

\subsection*{Camera-derived respiratory evidence and fixed observation bank}
The most important methodological lesson is that the camera-derived observation
is part of the model, not just a preprocessing choice. Supplementary Table S2
therefore summarizes the eight active single-observation routes in a fixed
display order: optical flow, optical-flow bridge, depth-of-flow, depth-of-flow
bridge, linear Profile1D, quadratic Profile1D, cubic Profile1D, and Profile1D
consensus. This order is a table and figure convention, not a sequential
pipeline and not a claim that one observation is transformed into the next.

Three contrasts are central. First, raw optical flow and the optical-flow
bridge are both flow-derived, but they are not interchangeable. The raw signal
is closer to a velocity-like cue, whereas the bridge converts that cue toward a
displacement-compatible respiratory trajectory and is therefore mainly useful
for timing support in the current framework. Second, raw depth-of-flow and the
depth-of-flow bridge are also distinct. The raw signal is a burst-like
motion-energy count, whereas the bridge recovers only a coarse signed
motion-centroid trajectory. Third, the Profile1D branch is explicitly split
into a conservative fundamental route, two harmonic morphology routes, and a
consensus route that checks whether the linear, quadratic, and cubic profile
interpretations agree.
\FloatBarrier

These constructed observations are included for information content and
coordinate compatibility, not for cosmetic smoothing. When a constructed route
fails, that failure is also evidence: it indicates that the current camera
observation set does not contain a clean enough respiratory projection for a
universal waveform claim.

\subsection*{Candidate evidence views and observation construction}
The eight motion-observation classes above are instantiated as persistent
single-observation routes for diagnosis and for the representative
baseline/comparator rows. The integrated PARH-OSSM readout reported in
Tables~\ref{tab:cohface_t3} and \ref{tab:cohface_t4} uses the final
observation-law package rather than a post hoc best route. Raw
Farneb\"ack optical flow is computed from the median vertical component of dense
motion between adjacent chest-ROI frames~\cite{Farneback2003}; the bridge,
depth-of-flow, and Profile1D variants then provide the complementary
construction routes summarized in Supplementary Table S2.

For that reason, preprocessing is part of the observation definition rather
than a generic denoising block. The raw extractor output, the inference-path
signal, and the helper-path signal all have different meanings. PARH-OSSM
keeps raw, bridged, band-limited, sign-coded, current, and helper views
separate until the adaptive observation law estimates reliability and nuisance.
The integrated readout is therefore not a hard choice of one observation for
all clips. It is a bounded integration of candidate evidence into a shared
respiratory state, with diagnostics that expose when the available evidence is
too weak. The COHFACE evidence also shows why constructed observations deserve
to be treated as first-class measurement routes: raw optical flow and the
optical-flow bridge invert the usual rate-versus-waveform ranking across the
same underlying source, which would be impossible to explain if observation
construction were merely cosmetic preprocessing.

The failure-case panels reported in Results are drawn from these persistent
manifests rather than manual inspection.

\subsection*{Adaptive observation law and label-free summaries}
The adaptive observation law is not a hard selector that chooses the best
observation after seeing the respiratory reference. For each target video, the
pipeline first constructs multiple motion-observation classes and several
views of each class, including raw, detrended, band-limited, sign/event,
robustly scaled, current-path, and helper views. These views are not separate
final models and they are not an ordered preprocessing cascade. They are
parallel evidence channels used to ask whether a camera-derived cue carries
plausible timing, morphology, or nuisance-dominated content.

The reliability evidence is computed without using the respiratory reference
label. The retained features measure whether independent
observation classes support a similar respiratory rate or phase, whether the evidence survives
simple preprocessing changes, whether half/double-rate aliasing is likely, and
whether nuisance evidence or abstention pressure is high. The resulting video-level and
motion-class reliability priors are then used to modulate how strongly the
constructed evidence affects the state update and the bounded oscillatory readout.
The provenance tables store video identifiers and reliability scores for
reproducibility, but those storage fields are not scientific variables in the
model. Oracle or reference-derived columns are explicitly removed before the
evaluation run.

Operationally, the reliability law has three fixed stages. First, each
candidate observation view contributes label-free features: cross-source
rate agreement, preprocessing invariance, event-timing support, bridge timing
support, rate/phase support, morphology support, ROI observability, harmonic
alias risk, nuisance evidence, and abstention pressure. Second, these features
are clipped to $[0,1]$ and combined with fixed coefficients into state-role
responsibilities for the fundamental timing anchor, second-harmonic scaffold,
baseline support, residual support, and abstention. For example, agreement is a
fixed weighted combination of group support, macro timing, bridge timing, and
rate/phase support; observability is the geometric mean of ROI and preprocessing
support; and harmonic ambiguity discounts the fundamental-rate responsibility
rather than being treated as a simple failure flag. Third, the component
responsibilities modulate $\pi_t$, $Q_{\mathrm{osc},t}$,
$Q_{\mathrm{aper},t}$, and the bounded $z_{\mathrm{osc}}$ readout. The exact
feature columns, coefficients, and source files are listed in the Supplementary
Methods and release audit files; no target respiratory waveform or
post hoc oracle source is used in these computations.

In the evaluated path, COHFACE uses combined label-free reliability evidence
on the readout side, whereas MAHNOB-HCI uses separate state-component and
readout reliability evidence. These scores softly modulate
observation trust, effective measurement uncertainty, and the bounded
$z_{\mathrm{osc}}$ readout inside the PARH-OSSM update. They do not switch the
system to the non-adaptive OSSM-KF comparator, and they do not select a
trial-specific best waveform source from the ground truth. Respiratory ground
truth is used for evaluation and for after-the-fact oracle/failure analyses
only. When the label-free evidence is ambiguous, the intended behaviour is
conservative: inflate uncertainty or preserve the existing readout rather than
force a winner-take-all source switch.

This design also defines the learning boundary. The evaluated system is not an
end-to-end supervised waveform predictor and it is not a dataset-specific
hyperparameter sweep. The fixed observation operators, resonant state
definition, Kalman/RTS inference equations, and metric routing are not learned
per target dataset. What is calibrated weakly is the label-free reliability
evidence that controls how strongly already constructed evidence
should affect the state update and readout. The complete COHFACE and
MAHNOB-HCI evaluation cohorts are used to keep all available
baseline, comparator, and proposed rows traceable; target labels are not used
to choose a trial's best observation or waveform source in the evaluated
system.

\subsection*{Role-specific weights and adaptive state scales}
The role-specific weight matrix $W_t$ is the interface between the candidate
evidence pool and the latent state. Its rows correspond to candidate
camera-derived views, and its columns correspond to respiratory roles:
fundamental oscillatory drive, second-harmonic asymmetry, baseline/projection
drift, and aperiodic residual structure. A strong view can therefore support
one role without being forced to support every readout. This is why the
adaptive law is a responsibility assignment, not a source selector: the same
candidate evidence pool can be reused differently for rate, morphology, and
diagnostics.

After those role responsibilities are defined, PARH-OSSM separates three
internal scores that control trust and state flexibility:
\begin{equation}
q_{\mathrm{obs},t} \rightarrow \pi_t,\qquad
q_{\mathrm{dyn},t} \rightarrow Q_{\mathrm{osc},t},\qquad
1-q_{\mathrm{osc},t} \rightarrow Q_{\mathrm{aper},t}.
\end{equation}
Here $q_{\mathrm{obs},t}$ summarizes how much the current observation should be
trusted before seeing the innovation, and it is converted to the prior trust
term $\pi_t$. The score $q_{\mathrm{dyn},t}$ controls how freely the
oscillatory state is allowed to move, and $q_{\mathrm{osc},t}$ separates
oscillatory support from aperiodic or residual content. The effective
measurement variance is then adjusted through
\(
\Reff = R_t / \pi_t
 \),
while Student-$t$ downweighting remains a posterior safeguard through
$\lambda_t$. Mehra-style adaptive measurement noise
remains the primary scale-setting mechanism~\cite{Mehra1970}; the quality
scores modulate the prior trust and process noise around that baseline.

\subsection*{PARH-OSSM latent respiratory state and inference}
PARH-OSSM uses the latent state
\begin{equation}
\state_t =
\left[
h_{c,t}^{(1)},\,
h_{s,t}^{(1)},\,
h_{c,t}^{(2)},\,
h_{s,t}^{(2)},\,
b_t,\,
\dot{b}_t,\,
r_t,\,
\dot{r}_t
\right]^\top,
\end{equation}
where $(h^{(1)}, h^{(2)})$ are the fundamental and second-harmonic oscillatory
components, $(b_t,\dot{b}_t)$ is a slow baseline block, and
$(r_t,\dot{r}_t)$ is an aperiodic residual block. The observation equation is
\begin{equation}
\obs_t = H \state_t + v_t, \qquad
H = [1,\,0,\,1,\,0,\,1,\,0,\,1,\,0].
\end{equation}

The superscript identifies the harmonic order. The first harmonic represents
the dominant respiratory rhythm, while the second harmonic represents
within-cycle asymmetry such as unequal inspiration and expiration shapes. The
subscripts $c$ and $s$ denote real quadrature coordinates, not separate
physiological measurements; the sine coordinate stores phase so that the
oscillator can rotate smoothly through amplitude changes and short periods of
weak observation support. The baseline pair $(b_t,\dot b_t)$ captures slow
projection or posture drift, and the residual pair $(r_t,\dot r_t)$ captures
aperiodic respiratory structure that is not well represented by a narrow-band
oscillator. The default observation row $H$ states that a scalar
camera-derived motion cue may contain contributions from the oscillatory,
baseline, and residual coordinates. In the adaptive observation law, the
effective trust in that cue is then modulated rather than assumed to be fixed.

The harmonic blocks use rotation-matrix dynamics conditioned on an external
frequency estimate, the baseline block follows a constant-velocity model, and
the residual block follows a damped random walk. Because the model remains
linear conditional on the external frequency, standard Kalman filtering and
Rauch--Tung--Striebel smoothing remain valid~\cite{Kalman1960,Sarkka2013,RauchTungStriebel1965}.
Thus PARH-OSSM is not a filter-free alternative to Kalman filtering. Its
contribution is the physiological state decomposition and observation-aware
trust law wrapped around that inference machinery, whereas OSSM-KF removes
those PARH-specific components and keeps only the fixed oscillator comparator.
The derivative coordinates $\dot{b}_t$ and $\dot{r}_t$ are inertial state
coordinates rather than camera channels: $\dot{b}_t$ allows slow drift to
continue smoothly instead of being repeatedly absorbed by the oscillator, and
$\dot{r}_t$ allows non-harmonic respiratory residual structure to persist
briefly instead of being treated as independent frame noise.

\subsection*{Decoupled readouts for rate and waveform morphology}
PARH-OSSM uses two readouts derived directly from the latent coordinates, and
we name the non-oscillatory complement explicitly:
\begin{equation}
z_{\mathrm{osc},t}
    = h_{c,t}^{(1)} + h_{c,t}^{(2)},
\qquad
z_{\mathrm{morph},t}
    = b_t + r_t,
\qquad
z_{\mathrm{full},t}
    = z_{\mathrm{osc},t} + z_{\mathrm{morph},t}.
\end{equation}
The added symbol $z_{\mathrm{morph}}$ is not a third exported performance
signal. It names the baseline--residual complement that turns the oscillatory
anchor into the full respiratory waveform. The oscillatory readout
$z_{\mathrm{osc}}$ carries the dominant rhythm and is the natural source for
rate and breath-timing analysis. The full readout $z_{\mathrm{full}}$ adds slow
trajectory drift and aperiodic respiratory departures back to that anchor and is
therefore the waveform compared against the reference morphology. Both causal
and smoothed versions of the exported readouts are retained as trial-level
outputs.

This decomposition is central to the study design because rate, breath timing,
and trajectory morphology are not the same target. A camera cue can preserve a
dominant respiratory frequency while losing the trajectory shape, or it can
carry morphology while introducing scale, lag, or residual content that harms
rate estimation. For that reason, PARH-OSSM does not force all evaluations
through one waveform-derived objective. Instead, the model asks whether a video
contains enough oscillatory information for rate and enough structured
respiratory-form information for waveform analysis.

\subsection*{Physiological rationale and diagnostic scope}
The model structure is not arbitrary. A second harmonic is included because
respiratory motion is usually asymmetric over each breath cycle even when the
dominant rate is stable; forcing that asymmetry into a purely sinusoidal state
invites harmonic confusion and pushes morphology into innovation residuals.
The baseline block is needed because camera-derived chest motion carries slow
projection drift and posture-related movement that are not themselves the
respiratory rhythm. The residual block exists for a different reason: it is the
state-space location where irregular respiratory content should live when the
oscillator support weakens, including amplitude transients or breath-shape
departures that are still respiratory but not well explained by the harmonic
submodel.

This decomposition also clarifies the evaluation policy. Rate metrics should be
computed from $z_{\mathrm{osc}}$, because rate is an oscillatory quantity.
Waveform-fidelity metrics should be computed from $z_{\mathrm{full}}$, because
the full motion trace is the object of comparison when baseline and aperiodic
content matter. The explicit complement $z_{\mathrm{morph}}=b_t+r_t$ makes that
separation visible: it is the morphology/trajectory content that must be added
to the oscillator before waveform fidelity is evaluated. That separation is one
of the central conceptual
distinctions from single-output respiratory estimators, where rate and waveform
interpretations are easily conflated. It also follows directly from the
state-space design principle above: once the state variables are assigned
distinct physiological roles, the observation targets and evaluation metrics
must remain aligned with those roles rather than collapsing everything back to
a single generic output.

\subsection*{Verified model scope and reproducibility boundary}
The evaluated PARH-OSSM system includes the eight-dimensional state, the
$K=2$ harmonic structure, baseline and residual blocks, dual readouts,
warm-up-only frequency initialisation, external timing-evidence updates,
causal and smoothed outputs, and per-sample diagnostics for $R_t$, $\pi_t$,
$\lambda_t$, $\nu_t$, $q_{\mathrm{obs},t}$,
$q_{\mathrm{dyn},t}$, and $q_{\mathrm{osc},t}$. These diagnostics are part of
the scientific output because they expose whether the model is using the
oscillatory, harmonic, baseline, or residual pathways, and whether the
available observation is trustworthy enough for rate or waveform analysis.

The results should therefore be read as evidence for a bounded
observation-aware state-space framework, not as a fully learned
multi-observation covariance model for every source and time segment. The
fixed observation operators and candidate views provide structured evidence;
the label-free reliability scores modulate how that evidence enters the
state update and readout; and the PARH latent state keeps the physiological
roles explicit. Helper-only and helper-blend variants are treated as
diagnostic context rather than as the proposed method.

To make the shared-versus-adaptive observation story testable at the state
interface, the system also records diagnostic observation summaries,
bootstrap latent drives for the harmonic, baseline, and residual blocks,
local adaptor parameters, and a global observation row in the
adaptive-resonator diagnostic path. These objects are not primary performance
outputs. They are retained so that the reader can inspect how the observation
interface drives the latent states. Supplementary Fig. S6 visualizes these
traces, and Supplementary Fig. S7 summarizes confidence and agreement
diagnostics that motivate separate oscillatory and full-waveform readouts. Both
figures are mechanistic diagnostics rather than competing final-package model
claims.

OSSM-KF remains a non-adaptive comparator: it uses conventional Kalman/RTS
inference, but does not contain the PARH observation law, dual-readout routing,
morphology complement, or calibration-diagnostic pathways.

\subsection*{Datasets and evaluation scope}
COHFACE contains 160 recordings from 40 subjects in a controlled
lab setting~\cite{COHFACE2017}. MAHNOB-HCI provides a more heterogeneous
emotion-elicitation environment with longer and higher-frame-rate
recordings~\cite{MAHNOB2012}. PARH-OSSM uses COHFACE
as a regular, do-no-harm regime and MAHNOB-HCI as an irregular regime where
the residual pathway should matter more.

The main quantitative layer is a predefined complete-cohort evaluation for each
dataset. The purpose of this evaluation is to report the
PARH-OSSM readout on every eligible trial while preserving traceable per-trial
diagnostics and explicitly marking any comparator computability limits.
Single-observation comparison results are retained as diagnostic context rather than used as
post hoc best-observation selection. The quantitative scope is therefore
cross-dataset but deliberately bounded.
Figure~\ref{fig:dataset_observation_regime} compares the clean COHFACE
regime with the harder MAHNOB-HCI regime using the same observation-stage EDA
axes. It shows why the observation class should be treated as part of the
modelled measurement channel rather than as a generic interchangeable
preprocessing choice: observation/stage effects that look usable in COHFACE become
weaker, less correlated, or nuisance-dominated in MAHNOB-HCI.

Supplementary Table S1 defines the evidence boundary for every
dataset considered here. COHFACE and MAHNOB-HCI support
the main real-data waveform/rate claims. V4V is useful only for external
rate-only validation because it does not contain a raw respiratory waveform.
SCAMPS is useful only as a controlled synthetic diagnostic or optional
pretraining source and must not be mixed into real-data performance claims.
The evaluation package therefore includes a mandatory external weak-evidence
analysis and a four-dataset distribution EDA rather than treating these datasets
as additional main benchmark rows. V4V contributes only its RR-label
distribution and rate-only scope. SCAMPS contributes synthetic
$d_{\mathrm{br}}$ breathing-signal coverage and a controlled peak-rate
distribution computed under the dataset-scope assumptions. Neither dataset
contributes hidden training, target tuning, or real waveform main-table metrics.
For respiratory estimation, a dataset may contain a breathing label while still
lacking the camera-observation conditions needed to support morphology or
deployment claims.

The complete dataset-and-claim-scope table is provided as Supplementary Table S1.
Figure~\ref{fig:dataset_observation_regime} is placed at the start of Results so
that the clean/hard dataset regime and the V4V/SCAMPS evidence boundary are
visible before performance claims are introduced.

\subsection*{Evaluation protocol}
We use role-routed quantitative sources rather than a
single waveform-derived score for all claims.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item Table~\ref{tab:cohface_t3} reports rate accuracy from the integrated
      oscillatory rate source.
\item Table~\ref{tab:cohface_t4} reports waveform fidelity from
      $z_{\mathrm{full}}$ in smoothed mode.
\item The supplementary calibration table reports PARH diagnostics plus
      limited OSSM-KF stability context. NIS and $\lambda_t$ rows are
      available only for PARH-OSSM because those adaptive arrays are not part of
      the standard comparator.
\item The T4b strict-waveform and T4c cycle-timing companions are both derived
      from the strict waveform raw metrics. These rows are not substitutes for
      the aligned morphology table; they are the harder zero-lag,
      unit-preserving checks.
\end{itemize}

Every main-table value is linked to a metric-provenance record containing the
dataset, method label, result directory, metric file, trial count, and whether
the row is main-table or diagnostic-only. COHFACE main-table rows share the same
160 trials. MAHNOB-HCI main PARH rows use all 525 tail-aligned trials, while
the representative P1D quad baseline and OSSM-KF comparator are available on
488 trials; common-488 paired deltas are therefore reported separately as
supplementary statistics. Target respiratory labels are used for evaluation and
after-the-fact oracle analyses only, not for label-free reliability calibration or
trial-specific source selection.

This differs intentionally from the evaluation protocol adopted in the
resPyre benchmark review~\cite{Boccignone2025Review}. In that benchmark,
each method is first treated as a respiratory-waveform estimator and
respiration rate is then derived from the estimated waveform by spectral peak
selection. That design is appropriate for comparing heterogeneous extractors in
a common benchmark pipeline, but it is not sufficient for the PARH-OSSM
question because it collapses oscillatory-rate behaviour and full-waveform
behaviour back onto a single output. We therefore keep the benchmark spirit
and datasets, but change the evaluation routing to match the latent semantics:
rate accuracy is evaluated through the oscillatory output when the model
provides it, waveform fidelity is evaluated through the full output, and
calibration diagnostics are reported separately.

The waveform metrics are intentionally split into two tiers. The main waveform
table is morphology-friendly: bandpass filtering to the respiratory band,
per-signal z-scoring, cross-correlation alignment, then whole-trial
concordance correlation coefficient, waveform MAE, and normalised
DTW~\cite{LinCCC1989}. This is appropriate when the question is whether the
recovered signal preserves respiratory morphology after removing trivial affine
and lag mismatch. Calibration metrics are computed from per-frame
diagnostics arrays when present, with frame logs used only for supplementary
failure and uncertainty fields.

We also generate a stricter supplementary reconstruction check from the
trial-level outputs. That strict protocol keeps the respiratory-band
filter but removes per-trial z-scoring and cross-correlation alignment, uses a
zero-lag resampled overlap only, and reports strict CCC/MAE/RMSE/DTW together
with cycle-level peak, trough, and peak-to-peak timing errors. The evaluation
boundary is therefore explicit: the main waveform table is an aligned
morphology comparison, while the strict table and cycle-level table are the
harder tests of unit-preserving waveform reconstruction.

All overlay figures and case studies are drawn from predefined case-selection
manifests rather than manual inspection. Additional confirmatory comparisons
should likewise be run as paired analyses on regenerated result records.

\subsection*{Implementation verification and reproducibility checks}
The reported tables are generated from persistent trial-level output records,
not from manually transcribed values. The verification package checks that the
model head, wrapper, evaluation scripts, table-ready CSV files, figure
manifests, and manuscript numbers agree before submission. It also aggregates
the per-sample diagnostic arrays from the trial payloads to separate integrated
PARH-OSSM performance from diagnostic single-observation evidence. These checks
support the interpretation that MAHNOB-HCI is primarily an observability and
target-shift limitation rather than a simple filter-instability failure.


\section*{Results}
\subsection*{Evaluation scope and evidence routing}
Quantitative claims are restricted to the predefined evaluation
cohorts, and each table states its evidence layer. The main performance tables
compare three explicitly tracked sources. The first is a pre-specified fixed
direct-observation baseline (P1D quad), the second is the corresponding
OSSM-KF comparator, and the third is the integrated PARH-OSSM run. The
complete eight-observation comparison across the direct baselines, OSSM-KF,

than used to select a post hoc best observation source.
This scope matters because we do not claim that every observation class
generalizes universally. The claim is that the proposed adaptive
observation-to-state framework is auditable, performs strongly when respiratory
evidence is present, and exposes when the camera-observation bank is
insufficient. The complete-cohort evidence layer is therefore not a
method-specific cherry-pick: it reports all eligible PARH trials and explicitly exposes
baseline/comparator computability limits. The dataset-level claims remain
limited to what these evaluation records and their supplementary provenance
support.

\subsection*{Calibration diagnostics}
The calibration diagnostics summarise a limited but important boundary.
PARH-OSSM stores NIS and Student-$t$ adaptation diagnostics, while
OSSM-KF provides comparable stability duration but not the
PARH-specific NIS, $\lambda_t$, or adaptive reliability arrays. MAHNOB-HCI is not
explained by gross state-space divergence: NIS remains close to one and
Student-$t$ weights do not collapse. The failure is therefore better read as
limited respiratory evidence plus reference/observability risk, not as an
unstable Kalman-style update. The complete calibration diagnostics are retained

\subsection*{Ablation evidence and failure decomposition}
The rate-source decomposition and
Table~\ref{tab:observability_taxonomy} first ask why the evaluated system does
not close MAHNOB-HCI. The rate-source decomposition shows that COHFACE has a strong native
timing source and that the reported track is a conservative but still
high-correlation readout. In MAHNOB-HCI, however, even the best diagnostic rate

final track. The failure taxonomy shows that this oracle room is itself

the hard regime is not best described as a missing hyperparameter. It is an
observability problem: the current fixed OF/DoF/Profile1D sources do not always

The COHFACE operator-alignment ablation asks a narrow but important question:
should respiratory timing and waveform morphology be forced through the same
output? The answer is no. A waveform-specialized expert can match aligned
morphology, but it loses rate. The decoupled readout preserves the strong
oscillatory path while retaining the same aligned waveform CCC and strict CCC.
This is the cleanest numeric support for evaluating rate from $z_{\mathrm{osc}}$
and waveform morphology from
$z_{\mathrm{full}}=z_{\mathrm{osc}}+z_{\mathrm{morph}}$.

\section*{Discussion}
\subsection*{Why PARH-OSSM gains are still limited}
The combined COHFACE and MAHNOB-HCI evidence narrows the problem. The latent
decomposition is no longer the dominant failure mode. The present framework has
an active second harmonic, non-saturated oscillatory-support scores, usable
baseline and residual states, corrected rate routing, and observation-dependent
gains that would not appear if the extra states were dormant. The remaining
bottleneck is the observation equation and the label-free evidence available to
that equation. NIS remains near unity and Student-$t$ weights do not collapse,
which reinforces that the hard-regime failures are driven by observation
quality rather than gross filter instability.

COHFACE and MAHNOB-HCI expose that bottleneck differently. In clean COHFACE,
the integrated system preserves strong rate and aligned morphology. In
irregular MAHNOB-HCI, the same framework remains calibrated but the final rate
correlation and waveform fidelity are weak. This means the proposed state-space
organization is not enough by itself: a hard target regime also needs richer or
more reliable respiratory observations.

\subsection*{What the evidence supports}
Three claims are now defensible.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item Observation construction matters. Raw optical flow and the optical-flow
bridge come from the same source but lead to materially different
rate-versus-waveform trade-offs.
\item Observation roles are now identifiable. Quadratic and cubic Profile1D
routes are waveform-primary harmonic cues, the optical-flow bridge is a
rate-oriented constructed cue, Profile1D consensus adds rate-supporting
agreement evidence, and raw/bridged depth-of-flow routes remain auxiliary cues.
\item The hard-regime failure is measurable rather than opaque. MAHNOB-HCI does
not support strong waveform claims under the current observation bank, but the
observability taxonomy identifies when the bank has little oracle room and when
source-selection evidence exists but remains insufficient for deployable
source routing.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Current boundaries and next observation requirements}
Several boundaries still separate the present system from a fully
closed adaptive multi-observation state-space model.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item PARH-OSSM is an integrated but bounded observation-law system. It is
not a fully general time-local multi-observation matrix $H_{m,t}$ with explicit
source covariance for every observation.
\item The strict reconstruction tables show that aligned morphology is not
yet the same as true waveform recovery. The present framework can line up breath
shape after normalisation and lag correction, but it still fails badly when
gain, offset, and zero-lag timing must all be correct at once.
\item The present label-free reliability features are not sufficient for
deployment-style routing. They can reject some within-source consistency
patterns, but they still confuse repeated bridge-like waveforms with genuinely
recoverable respiratory morphology on difficult MAHNOB-HCI trials.
\item Comparator symmetry is incomplete because OSSM-KF is intentionally a
stripped-down resonator-plus-Kalman comparator. It shares the broad
state-space inference machinery, but it does not save the same per-frame
calibration arrays, posterior source roles, or posterior frequency uncertainty
fields as PARH-OSSM.
\item The residual branch is more interpretable than before, but it is still a
diagnosed leftover pathway rather than a separately validated physiological
event branch.
\item Multi-observation fusion is only partially closed. The integrated readout uses
bounded integration and explicit source analyses, but the next-generation model
should make every source's role, covariance, and low-observability decision
explicit inside one observation equation.
\end{itemize}

This boundary also clarifies how the framework should interact with rPPG and
deep learning. rPPG-derived respiratory modulation, learned motion fields, or
learned ROI-quality maps are natural additional observation operators, not
conceptual competitors to the state/readout decomposition~\cite{Gwak2024MultimodalBreathing,Du2021WeaklySupervisedRPPGRespiration}.
A future learned front end should be judged by whether it produces label-free
respiratory evidence and uncertainty that can enter the observation law without
using target labels at test time. Conversely, the state-space layer should keep
the timing, morphology, baseline, residual, calibration, and low-observability
diagnostics explicit, so that a deep model does not simply hide the same
failure modes inside an uninterpretable waveform output.

\subsection*{Limitations and next steps}
The current analysis no longer has an incomplete MAHNOB-HCI cohort limitation:
both major datasets are evaluated over their predefined cohorts. The remaining limitations
are scientific.

First, the integrated system is strong on COHFACE but still weak on
MAHNOB-HCI. That gap should not be explained away as a formatting artifact:
MAHNOB rate correlation is low, aligned waveform CCC is modest, and strict
waveform CCC is 0.000 in the complete-cohort table. Second, the
calibration diagnostics do not
show gross state divergence, so simply retuning a Kalman parameter is unlikely
to be the right scientific fix. Third, the label-free observability analysis shows
that many MAHNOB trials have little recoverable room inside the present
OF/DoF/Profile1D bank. The conclusion is that the framework is useful and
auditable, but the present observation bank is not sufficient for universal
camera-based respiratory reconstruction.

The next development stage should therefore prioritise four tasks. (1) Build
richer respiratory observations before adding more output-side gates, especially
phase-anchored morphology cues that survive person and environment shift. (2)
Make label-free reliability time-local, so trust can change within a recording
instead of being inferred mostly at the clip level. (3) Make the residual branch
identifiable as respiratory residual structure rather than a catch-all
observation-error absorber. (4) Promote multi-observation fusion only when each
source carries an explicit role, covariance, and low-observability flag; naive
source stacking would obscure the main lesson of the present analysis.
